Step 1: Self-Reflection

An honest analysis of my linguistic strengths, weaknesses, and expectations at the beginning of the English III course:

"My greatest strength is reading because I easily understand the general meaning of texts. My listening is good, but it could be better. However, speaking and writing are much more difficult for me. When I speak, I hesitate and forget words. I often have ideas on the tip of my tongue, but it seems my brain and mouth are not working properly, so I need more synonyms to express myself with more confidence. Finally, writing is the hardest skill. I hope to improve these weaknesses in this course."

Task 2: Exploration of Leadership Identity

Exploring leadership identity

I have chosen Academic Leadership as my primary profile because I truly enjoy guiding and supporting others in educational spaces. Although my husband is the main leader and financial provider in our home, and I do not enjoy participating in community activities, I have identified a meaningful intersection between academic and family leadership. As a mother, I must guide my daughter who is currently in third grade by explaining her homework, organizing her study schedule, and making sure she submits her assignments on time. In addition, in my university life, I feel a stronger passion for helping my peers achieve their academic goals. This selection aligns with Contreras & Garzón (2021), who observed that university students often focus their energy on academic preparation and personal development rather than local community politics. Furthermore, as the British Council (n.d.) explains in their study on cultural expectations, an academic leader functions best when acting as a supportive mentor who encourages shared goals rather than an authoritarian figure. Therefore, academic leadership allows me to combine my maternal responsibility with my educational passion in a natural way.

Regarding my leadership qualities, I have always valued responsibility, punctuality, and strong personal ethics. I consider myself an empathetic and generous leader who prefers active listening to understand what my team members can propose. As Hall & Foley (2003) emphasize in their study on leadership communication, a responsible leader must demonstrate ethical duties and should encourage active participation from everyone. In terms of methodology, I am much more creative when using educational technology than manual tasks because digital tools make learning more dynamic. This experience connects with Contreras & Garzón (2021), who highlight that adopting the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) helps users perceive digital tools as useful instruments that improve group performance. However, my most difficult challenge is managing my patience. While I can be extremely patient with classmates who show genuine effort, my patience runs out when people do not show commitment. A good leader must inspire enthusiasm, but team members must also contribute equal effort to reach success together.

Throughout my academic life, I have demonstrated leadership in different contexts and practical situations. In our UNAD virtual courses, I have guided my classmates through WhatsApp study groups by reminding them about webconferences, explaining task guidelines, and providing constructive feedback. Before I started studying at UNAD, I had already developed group leadership skills at Universidad de Sucre, where I led team projects, opened my home, and shared food, spaces, and study materials. To narrate these past experiences accurately, Learn English with Cambridge (2021) recommends combining the past simple and past perfect tenses to show sequence and progress in leadership actions. Furthermore, these collaborative actions agree with the British Council (n.d.), which explains that in low power distance environments, leaders act as coaches who encourage democratic decision-making instead of giving strict orders. Applying these mentoring strategies has helped me support both my university peers and my daughter's primary education effectively.

In conclusion, leadership is essential in my life because it helps orient people toward a common goal and generates positive impacts on everyone around us. Without clear leadership, our society would live in complete chaos, as structured guidance is always necessary to support progress and mutual growth. As Contreras & Garzón (2021) concluded in their research, true leadership requires adopting new technologies and organizational frameworks to contribute to the welfare and development of the group. In addition, Fajardo (2025a) notes that using appropriate linking words and open communication allows leaders to connect ideas clearly and foster strong teamwork. Whether in family or academic environments, being a leader means taking the initiative to empower others. Ultimately, my academic leadership profile has shown that when we share knowledge, show empathy, and use digital tools, we can build better learning spaces and inspire meaningful success.
